# Wedding Reference

Findings for Ananthula's Wedding chapter. All observations `source-derived`
(2026-08-09) unless tagged.

## Headline finding

**Three of four competitors have no wedding destination at all**, and the fourth
has a named campaign with no page behind it. Every one of them treats wedding as
a *photographic register* rather than a *commerce path*.

| Site | Wedding imagery | Wedding page | Bridal collection | Groom collection |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SISM | 2 of 6 heroes + named "Wedding Mahotsav" campaign | **No** | 87 items, **unlinked** | Via Bandhagala/Suits/Indo-Western |

| RSB | 2 heroes (bridal lehenga, ivory sherwani) | **No** | **None in taxonomy** | **None** — proxy categories only |

`inference` — this is the single largest unclaimed territory in the set. A South
Indian family retailer with a real wedding chapter would be alone in the market.

## BRIDE

**Observed treatments:**

- **CSM slide 3** (strongest bridal frame found): woman seated on a
  flower-garlanded swing in a pink/gold/green brocade Kanchipuram, full temple
  jewellery set (long haaram, choker, jhumkas, maang tikka, vaddanam);
  neoclassical colonnade, lily pond, swans, rose and bougainvillea garlands,
  sunset sky. Subject in the right third, logo placed in the left negative space.
  Soft painterly warm light.
- **SISM slide 6**: bride and groom tightly framed together in the **left third**,
  bride in a pink-and-gold Kanchipuram with red contrast blouse, temple jewellery;
  painted mandapam backdrop, hanging jasmine-and-rose garlands, right half left
  deliberately empty for type.
- **SISM slide 3**: two women in green-and-gold and purple-and-gold Kanchipurams,
  **pallu fanned across the floor to show the border** — the one frame in the
  entire study where the textile, not the face, is the subject.

**Recurring pattern:** garment family is always Kanchipuram/pattu; jewellery
intensity is high (full temple set); setting is a built mandapam or courtyard;
light is soft, warm, painterly; negative space is reserved on one side for type.

**Lessons to take (`inference`):**
- Reserve one side of frame for typography — all the good frames do this.
- Fan the pallu into the composition; the border is the product.
- Warm painterly light reads as ceremonial without going literal.

**What to avoid:**
- Jewellery intensity that makes the saree secondary — CSM's swing frame is
  beautiful but the haaram competes with the weave.
- Painted-canvas mandapam backdrops (SISM); they read as studio, not occasion.
- Ananthula's bride should be **quieter** than all of these: restrained temple
  jewellery, one light, real environment, emotional stillness over spectacle.

## GROOM

**Observed treatments:**

- **RSB slide 5** (strongest groom frame): two male models in ivory tone-on-tone
  wedding sherwanis, one with a draped dupatta, one in a layered open jacket;
  ivory Mughal-arch panelling with gold trellis, brass lanterns, floral urn;
  soft high-key warm bounce; pair on the left third, 40% of frame reserved for copy.
- **SISM slide 2**: two male models flanking a pale-pink arch, navy silk kurta with
  gold-embroidered Nehru jacket / slate floral kurta with white churidar; built set
  with terracotta gradient wall, palm frond, plinth.
- **CSM slide 4**: cream embroidered sherwani, hand on chest, bokeh floral arch.

**Recurring pattern:** North-Indian-leaning styling dominates —
sherwani, Mughal arch, Nehru jacket. Genuinely South Indian groom wear (silk
kurta with angavastram, pancha) appears only once (SISM's Wedding Mahotsav
banner: groom in cream silk kurta + angavastram).

**Lessons to take (`inference`):**
- The ivory/cream palette for groomswear photographs beautifully against warm
  architecture and separates cleanly from the bride's arakku.
- Reserving 40% of frame for copy works at hero scale.

**What to avoid — important for our brief:**
- **Sherwani-and-Mughal-arch as the default.** Three of four default to North
  Indian visual language. Ananthula's groom should wear silk kurta with
  angavastram in a Warangal-appropriate setting — that is both more honest and
  more differentiated.
- Turbans, garland-heavy styling, palace props.
- Ananthula's existing generated prompt (`docs/IMAGE_GENERATION_PROMPTS.md`
  asset 03) already specifies ivory raw-silk kurta + angavastram. **The research
  confirms that choice is correct and differentiating.**

## FAMILY

**Observed treatments:**

- **SISM Wedding Mahotsav banner**: three figures — couple at left (groom in cream
  silk kurta + angavastram, bride in gold/multi Kanchipuram with magenta blouse),
  celebrity re-used at right, two stacked cards in the centre. Same palace
  background as the celebrity banner, so the two read as one campaign.
- **CB hero**: nuclear family of four (father, son, daughter, mother) in a
  connected hand-holding chain, deliberately **non-coordinated** wardrobe —
  mother in emerald Kanjeevaram pattu, father in denim shirt, son in a neon
  hoodie, daughter in a Western floral dress + denim jacket. Cut out on a flat
  beige gradient with a red ribbon swoosh.

**Analysis of the CB family image (the only true family image in the study):**

- **Group size:** 4. **No grandparent — no third generation**, despite the brand
  claiming "50+ Years" and "Style for Every Generation".
- **Wardrobe differentiation:** deliberate and strategically smart — one frame
  says "we dress all four of you, however each of you dresses". `inference` —
  this is the best single idea found anywhere in the study.
- **Credibility: low.** Reads as composited/AI-generated: hard cut edges with no
  cast shadow onto the ground, identical flat light across four separately-lit
  subjects, no perspective in the ground plane, children's eyelines landing on
  nothing, four simultaneous identical-intensity smiles, geometrically perfect
  logo artwork sitting flat on the shopping-bag folds. The `alt` text reads
  `"Premium Diamond Necklace"`.
- **Product presence:** only one of four people wears anything from the brand's
  flagship category.

**Lessons to take (`inference`):**
- **Keep the non-coordination idea, execute it credibly.** Different wardrobes,
  one frame, one light source, real shadows, real eyelines.
- **Add the third generation.** The gap between "50+ years" and a nuclear-family
  hero is the category leader's biggest unforced error. A grandmother in frame
  closes it for free — and Ananthula's whole position is generational.
- **Put the flagship product on more than one person.** If Pattu is the house's
  authority, Pattu should appear on two generations in the same frame.

**What to avoid:**
- Cut-out-on-gradient treatment. Environment is credibility.
- Everyone smiling at the camera simultaneously.
- Matching-family gimmick (nobody does this, correctly).

## Ananthula constraints (`inference`, carried forward)

1. Remain recognisably **South Indian** — silk kurta and angavastram over
   sherwani; mandapam or courtyard over palace; temple jewellery restrained.
2. Wedding must feel **ceremonial and evening**; Family must feel **daylight and
   everyday-festive**. Different time of day, different ground colour, different
   framing — this is how the two chapters stay distinct.
3. Build the **commerce path** none of them built: bride → groom → family → one
   destination, with a working WhatsApp planning CTA. Ananthula's existing
   Wedding chapter already has this structure; the research validates it.
4. Do not add bridal certification, provenance or "trousseau package" claims
   without owner confirmation.
